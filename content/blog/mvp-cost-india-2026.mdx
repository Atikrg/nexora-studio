If you've asked three agencies to quote your MVP, you've probably received three numbers that look like they describe three different products. One says ₹2 lakh, another ₹9 lakh, a third won't give a number until after a "discovery workshop".

The ranges are wide because "MVP" means very different things to different people. This guide breaks down what an MVP actually costs in India in 2026, what moves the number up or down, and how to keep it lean without building something you'll have to throw away.

## The short answer

For a startup working with a small, senior team in India, these are realistic ranges in 2026:

| What you're building | Typical range | Timeline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Validation landing page + waitlist | ₹30,000 – ₹60,000 | 1–2 weeks |
| Credible startup website (5–8 pages, CMS) | ₹80,000 – ₹1,80,000 | 3–4 weeks |
| Clickable prototype (design sprint) | ₹50,000 – ₹1,00,000 | 2 weeks |
| MVP mobile app (Android + iOS, one core journey) | ₹2,50,000 – ₹6,00,000 | 6–10 weeks |
| Web platform / SaaS MVP | ₹3,00,000 – ₹8,00,000 | 8–12 weeks |
| Automation of one business process | ₹20,000 – ₹90,000 | 1–3 weeks |

Freelancers can be 30–50% cheaper. Large agencies are often 2–4x more expensive, mostly because of overheads like account managers, sales teams and office costs.

## What actually drives the cost

### 1. The number of user journeys

The single biggest cost driver isn't screens or features. It's how many **distinct journeys** the product supports. "Patient books a doctor and pays" is one journey. Add "doctor manages availability", "admin approves doctors" and "patient gets a refund", and you've roughly tripled the work.

**Rule of thumb:** each additional user role (customer, partner, admin) adds 30–60% to the build.

### 2. Real-time and offline features

Chat, live tracking, collaborative editing and offline sync are all expensive to build and test. If your MVP needs one of them, budget for it explicitly. If it doesn't need them *yet*, cut them.

### 3. Integrations

Payment gateways (Razorpay, Stripe), WhatsApp Business API, maps, KYC, accounting tools: each one is usually a few days of work, plus edge cases. Off-the-shelf integrations are cheap; custom ones with poor documentation are not.

### 4. Design depth

A clean, conventional UI built on a solid design system is fast. Custom illustration, complex animation and bespoke interactions add time. For most MVPs, clarity converts better than novelty anyway.

### 5. Who's on the team

You're paying for decisions as much as code. A senior developer who has built five similar products will often finish in half the time of a junior developer, and make far fewer expensive mistakes along the way.

## Six ways to spend less (without building throwaway code)

1. **Write down one sentence of success.** "100 paying users in 60 days" makes it obvious which features matter. Everything else goes on a "later" list.
2. **Start with a design sprint.** Spending ₹50–60k to test a clickable prototype with five real users regularly saves lakhs of rework.
3. **Use cross-platform for mobile.** React Native or Flutter ship Android and iOS from one codebase. Two native apps are rarely justified at MVP stage.
4. **Buy, don't build, the boring parts.** Authentication, payments, email, analytics and admin panels all have excellent off-the-shelf options.
5. **Automate operations instead of building features.** An n8n or Zapier workflow can replace a whole admin dashboard for your first few hundred users.
6. **Insist on a fixed scope and milestones.** A fixed price with 30/40/30 milestone payments protects you from both scope creep and disappearing teams.

## Red flags in a quote

- **No breakdown.** If you can't see what you're paying for, you can't cut scope intelligently.
- **100% upfront.** Reputable teams work on milestones.
- **No mention of ownership.** You should own the code, designs and app store accounts from day one.
- **"Unlimited revisions".** It sounds generous, but it usually means nobody has thought carefully about the scope.
- **No post-launch support.** Bugs show up after launch. Ask what's included and for how long.

## A sensible budget plan

If you're pre-seed with ₹10–15 lakh to spend on product, a sensible split looks like this:

- **10%** on validation: landing page, ads test, design sprint
- **50–60%** on the MVP build
- **15%** on launch: content, a small paid campaign, onboarding automation
- **15–25%** held back for iteration after launch, which is where most of the learning happens

The most expensive MVP is the one you build twice. Spend a little more time deciding *what* to build, and the build itself gets cheaper.
