Most startup landing pages fail for the same reason. They describe the product instead of the outcome, and they ask visitors to do too many things at once.

After building landing pages for SaaS, D2C, EdTech and HealthTech startups, we use the same nine-section structure almost every time. It isn't a template. It's the order in which a sceptical visitor's questions arise.

## The visitor's five questions

Every visitor, consciously or not, asks:

1. **What is this, and is it for me?** (5 seconds)
2. **Why should I believe you?**
3. **How does it work?**
4. **What does it cost, and what's the catch?**
5. **What do I do next?**

Each section below answers one of them.

## 1. Hero: outcome, audience, action

The hero has one job: make the right visitor think "this is for me" within five seconds.

**Formula:** *[Outcome] for [audience], without [pain].*

- ❌ "AI-powered invoicing platform"
- ✅ "Send a GST invoice in 30 seconds. Get paid 2x faster."

Include **one** primary call to action and an optional secondary one. Add a trust micro-line under the buttons ("No credit card · Cancel anytime · 2,000+ freelancers") because it removes friction exactly where it occurs.

## 2. Social proof strip

Logos of customers, investors or "as featured in". It goes immediately under the hero because it costs the visitor almost no attention and answers "is this legit?" early.

No logos yet? Use a specific number ("Used by 340 clinics") or a single strong testimonial.

## 3. The problem

Describe the pain in your customer's words, not yours. Read your support tickets, sales calls and Reddit threads, and steal their phrases. When a visitor reads their own frustration back, trust jumps.

Keep it to three or four pains. More becomes a rant.

## 4. The solution (in three steps)

Show how your product solves those specific pains. Three steps with simple visuals beat a feature grid:

1. Connect your bank account
2. Create an invoice from a template
3. Get paid by UPI or card, with automatic reminders

## 5. Features as benefits

Now you can list features, but write each one as a benefit:

| Feature | Benefit |
| --- | --- |
| Automatic payment reminders | Stop chasing clients on WhatsApp |
| GST-compliant templates | Never worry about a rejected invoice |
| Multi-currency | Bill international clients in their currency |

## 6. Proof, in depth

Testimonials with a **name, photo, role and a specific result** ("cut our collection time from 21 days to 6") are worth ten generic five-star quotes. If you have a case study, link to it here.

## 7. Pricing, or at least a starting price

Hiding prices doesn't make you look premium. It makes visitors leave to compare you with someone who doesn't hide them. If pricing is custom, show a starting price and what it includes.

## 8. FAQ: objection handling

Your FAQ isn't for trivia. It's for the reasons people *don't* buy. Common ones:

- Is my data safe?
- What if it doesn't work for me?
- Can I cancel?
- How long does setup take?

Bonus: FAQ content with FAQPage schema can win extra space in Google results.

## 9. Final call to action

Repeat the primary call to action with a fresh, confident headline and a reason to act now: a free trial, an onboarding call or a limited launch offer.

## The details that move conversion most

- **Speed:** aim for under 2 seconds on a mid-range Android phone over 4G. Every extra second costs sign-ups.
- **One primary action per section.** Choices slow people down.
- **Mobile first.** Check every section on a real phone. Most of your traffic will be there.
- **Readable type:** 16px minimum body text, lines no longer than about 65 characters.
- **Real screenshots over stock photos.** Show the product working.

## Pre-launch checklist

- [ ] Headline passes the 5-second test with someone outside your team
- [ ] One primary CTA, visible above the fold on mobile
- [ ] Analytics and conversion events set up
- [ ] Page loads in under 2 seconds (check with PageSpeed Insights)
- [ ] Meta title, description and Open Graph image set
- [ ] Form submissions tested end to end
- [ ] Privacy policy and contact details linked in the footer

A great landing page isn't clever. It's clear. Answer the visitor's questions in the order they ask them, and conversion follows.
